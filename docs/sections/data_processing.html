Data Processing Pipeline

The final ECG benchmark is built directly from raw MIT-BIH records. The preprocessing stage converts each retained annotation into a fixed beat-centered window, derives a paired frequency-domain view from the same saved window, and then constructs a leakage-aware balanced train/test split for downstream retrieval experiments.

Sampling rate 360 Hz
Window length 361 samples
FFT bins 181
Split policy Record-aware

Label-space design

The raw annotation stream contains many beat-level symbols together with markers that are not suitable as final retrieval classes. To obtain a cleaner benchmark, the retained endogenous beat symbols are collapsed into three coarse classes:

Coarse class Mapped symbols Meaning
N N, L, R, e, j normal, conduction-side, and escape-side endogenous beats
S A, a, J, S supraventricular ectopic beats
V V, E ventricular-origin beats

Fusion beats, paced beats, unclassifiable beats, flutter markers, blocked events, and artifact-related symbols are dropped from the final benchmark definition. This keeps the task aligned with a coarse endogenous beat retrieval problem rather than a heterogeneous annotation problem.

Window extraction policy

Each retained beat is extracted with a symmetric annotation-centered window of 180 samples before the annotation and 180 samples after it. At 360 Hz this produces a 361-sample event, corresponding to approximately 1.003 seconds of signal. The design keeps full local beat morphology together with a small amount of neighborhood context, which is useful for retrieval-oriented event matching.

Frequency-domain conversion

The frequency representation is derived from the saved time-domain window rather than from a separate preprocessing branch. Each channel is mean-centered per event and transformed with a one-sided FFT, storing 181 frequency bins in magnitude-phase form. This makes the time and frequency views strictly aligned at the event level.

Leakage-aware benchmark construction

Train and test are assigned at the record level, not at the beat level. Record identifiers are shuffled with a fixed seed, then accumulated according to the limiting class S until the desired split sizes are reached. This avoids train/test leakage through shared patient morphology.

Split Target per class Final total Construction rule
Train 2250 6750 record-aware selection, then even-per-record random downsampling
Test 250 750 record-aware selection from remaining records, then even-per-record random downsampling

Saved benchmark artifacts

  • time-domain windows with shape [N, 2, 361]
  • frequency-domain windows with shape [N, 2, 181, 2]
  • coarse labels N, S, V
  • sample metadata including record ID, center sample, and original symbol
  • split configuration, label map, and shared frequency bins
