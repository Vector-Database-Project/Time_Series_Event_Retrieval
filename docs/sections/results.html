Representation trend

Frequency-domain inputs are the strongest representation family in the current benchmark. The two strongest runs in the full matrix are both frequency-based GRP models, and the best frequency-based UMAP run is also stronger than the time-only UMAP runs. Mixed inputs are generally intermediate, while raw time-domain inputs are the weakest overall.

Dimension trend

The 64-dimensional setting is often as good as or better than the 128-dimensional setting. The best overall run, grp_frequency_064, outperforms its 128-dimensional counterpart, and the strongest UMAP result also comes from the 64-dimensional mixed setting. Larger embeddings therefore do not provide a consistent advantage here.

Neighborhood behavior across K

The effect of increasing K depends on the quality of the embedding neighborhood. For the strongest frequency-based GRP run, performance improves steadily from K = 1 to K = 10, indicating stable class-consistent neighborhoods. By contrast, several time-domain runs degrade as K increases, suggesting that their local neighborhoods become less pure when more neighbors are considered.

Interpretation

The final result of the benchmark is not that a more complex embedding model automatically improves retrieval. Instead, the stronger result is that representation choice matters more than model complexity in the tested setup. Frequency-domain preprocessing is the strongest of the examined views, and a simple random projection baseline preserves retrieval-relevant structure better than the UMAP-based alternatives under the current leakage-aware split.

Scope and limitation

These conclusions apply to the current ECG benchmark only: the coarse N/S/V label space, the balanced record-aware split, the tested time/frequency/mixed representations, the 64 and 128 dimensional settings, and the nearest-neighbor retrieval evaluator used here. The report therefore presents a controlled benchmark finding rather than a universal claim about all time-series retrieval problems.
