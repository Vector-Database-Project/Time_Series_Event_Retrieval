Conclusions

Main finding representation mattered more than model complexity
Strongest result grp_frequency_064
Representation trend frequency > mixed > time
Dimension trend 64 often matched or exceeded 128

Supported conclusions

  • Within the final leakage-aware benchmark, the strongest retrieval results are obtained from frequency-domain inputs rather than time-domain inputs.
  • The best overall method family in this setting is Gaussian Random Projection, not the learned UMAP variants.
  • The results do not support the claim that a more complex embedding model automatically improves retrieval quality for this benchmark.
  • The stronger conclusion is benchmark-specific, retrieval quality appears to depend more strongly on the chosen representation than on additional embedding complexity.
  • Larger embedding size does not provide a consistent advantage, since the 64-dimensional setting is frequently as strong as or stronger than 128 dimensions.

Possible explanations for the GRP result

  • A plausible explanation is that the frequency preprocessing already exposes useful class structure, so a simple linear projection is sufficient to preserve retrieval-relevant neighborhoods.
  • Gaussian Random Projection does not attempt to reshape the geometry aggressively. In this benchmark, that may help preserve distance relationships that are already useful for nearest-neighbor search.
  • UMAP and Supervised UMAP learn lower-dimensional manifolds from the training set, but the geometry they optimize is not identical to the final top-K Euclidean retrieval objective used for evaluation.
  • Under a stricter record-aware split, any method that depends more strongly on training-set neighborhood structure may be less able to benefit from source-specific regularities that do not transfer across records.
  • These points should be interpreted as plausible benchmark-level explanations rather than proven causal mechanisms.

Why frequency features may dominate

  • Frequency-domain representations may capture broader morphological structure in a more stable form than raw time-domain windows.
  • Compared with time-only inputs, spectral features may be less sensitive to small local alignment differences in the waveform, which can improve neighborhood consistency under retrieval.
  • The mixed representation improves over time-only inputs, but it does not surpass frequency alone, suggesting that adding more features does not automatically improve the geometry seen by the retrieval metric.
  • This pattern indicates that feature quality matters more than feature quantity in the present benchmark.

Evaluation dependence

Benchmark component Interpretive consequence
Euclidean nearest-neighbor retrieval The final comparison is tied to L2 distance in the embedding space, which may favor methods that preserve linear distance structure more directly.
Top-K majority voting Performance depends not only on single-neighbor accuracy but also on the local class composition of the retrieved neighborhood.
Macro-averaged metrics The reported summaries emphasize class-balanced behavior rather than performance dominated by the largest class alone.
  • For that reason, the observed advantage of GRP should be interpreted relative to this evaluation design rather than as a universal ranking of embedding methods.
  • A different distance function or retrieval rule could change the relative ordering of methods.
These conclusions apply only to the present ECG retrieval benchmark, the coarse N/S/V label space, the record-aware split, the tested time, frequency, and mixed representations, the 64- and 128-dimensional settings, and the nearest-neighbor evaluation protocol used here. The section therefore presents a benchmark-specific interpretation, not a general claim about all time-series retrieval problems.
